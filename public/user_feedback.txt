Dev Notes:

🎯 What You’ll Be Able to Calculate

From just this lightweight instrumentation:

Confidence delta

Psychological safety ratio

Repeat intent

Actual repeat rate

Feedback clarity rate

Recruiter adoption friction

Recruiter positioning distribution

Recruiter perception of preparedness lift

That’s more than enough for Phase 1.

Now here’s the subtle optimization layer:

If someone answers “Not helpful” or “Evaluative,”
do you want to trigger a short follow-up question in that moment?

Because that increases insight…
but slightly decreases response rate.

Which tradeoff do you prefer:

Maximum volume
or
Slightly lower volume, richer signal?

What would it be possible to know in a follow up question if their response is not what's desired?

Now we’re in the interesting territory.

When someone gives an “undesired” response (Not helpful, Felt evaluative, Not aligned, etc.), you have a very narrow window to learn something valuable before they mentally move on.

The follow-up should not be:

“Please explain.”

That kills response rate.

It should be:

Structured.
Quick.
Diagnostic.

The purpose is not to collect essays.
The purpose is to isolate which system variable failed.

Let’s break this down by failure type.

1️⃣ If They Say: “Not Helpful”

What you want to know:

Was it a clarity problem?
A relevance problem?
A depth problem?
A tone problem?

So your follow-up should be:

“What was missing?”

Not specific enough

Too generic

Too harsh

Too long

Didn’t match my role

That tells you exactly what to improve.

You don’t need prose.
You need categorization.

2️⃣ If They Say: “Somewhat Evaluative” or “Definitely Evaluative”

This is your psychological safety alarm.

You need to know:

Was it recruiter visibility?
Tone?
Scoring?
UI language?

Follow-up:

“What made it feel evaluative?”

The scores

The wording

Knowing recruiters can see it

It felt like a test

Other

If “Knowing recruiters can see it” shows up frequently,
that’s strategic signal.

If “The scores” shows up,
you might reconsider showing scores even to candidates.

This one is important.

3️⃣ If They Say: “Not Aligned with My Interview”

You need to know if fidelity failed at:

Stage selection?
Role parsing?
Difficulty level?

Follow-up:

“What felt off?”

Wrong stage

Too easy

Too difficult

Not relevant to my role

Too generic

Now you know where your JD-to-question pipeline is leaking.

4️⃣ If They Say: “Would Not Use Again”

That’s serious.

Follow-up:

“Why not?”

Too long

Not useful

Felt uncomfortable

Technical issues

Already confident

This helps you separate UX issues from value perception.

Important Design Rule

Only show follow-up after “negative” or “mixed” responses.

And:

One question only.

Multiple choice only.

Optional.

No typing required.

If you ask more than one, drop-off spikes.

What This Buys You Strategically

Instead of just knowing:

“15% found it not helpful.”

You’ll know:

60% of those thought it was too generic.

20% thought it was too harsh.

10% thought it was irrelevant.

That turns vague dissatisfaction into actionable product insight.

Now Let’s Zoom Out

You’re building a skill builder.

When negative signals show up, they usually cluster around:

Safety

Fidelity

Feedback precision

If your follow-ups map directly to those three axes, you can iterate intelligently without overbuilding analytics.
